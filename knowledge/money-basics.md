# Money basics: budget, safety net, debt, insurance

Headline figures (tax tariff, social insurance values, pension values, subsidies, allowances) were checked against sources on 11 November 2026. Rules of thumb and long-standing legal rules come from general knowledge and should be confirmed before anyone relies on them.

## Budget and accounts

- **Know the three numbers:** net income, fixed costs, and what is left. Most plans fail on a guessed spending figure; three months of real bank statements beat any estimate.
- **Guide values:** about 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% saving and debt repayment. In expensive cities rent pushes needs higher; then the saving rate has to come out of wants.
- **Pay yourself first:** standing orders for savings and investments on payday, not what is left at month end.
- **Mehrkontenmodell:** current account for fixed costs, a second account or sub-account for spending money, Tagesgeld for the emergency fund and planned purchases, Depot for investing.
- **Easy savings:** switch electricity, gas, mobile and internet contracts after the minimum term; review insurance yearly; cancel unused subscriptions; change Krankenkasse if the Zusatzbeitrag is high.

## Emergency fund (Notgroschen)

| Situation | Target |
|---|---|
| Employee, secure job, no dependants | 3 months of spending |
| Family, single earner, homeowner, old car | 4–6 months |
| Self-employed, commission income, landlord | 6–12 months |

- Keep it on Tagesgeld: available within a day, deposit-protected up to €100,000 per person per bank.
- It is insurance, not an investment. Do not chase return with it.
- Build the first month's worth before anything else, then continue in parallel with employer and state subsidies.

## Debt

**Order of attack**
1. Arrears on rent, energy, health insurance and tax: these threaten housing and cover. Deal with them first and talk to the creditor.
2. Overdraft (Dispo) and credit-card balances: typically 10–15% interest or more.
3. Instalment loans above about 5–6%.
4. Car and study loans at low rates: pay as agreed.
5. Mortgage: see `property.md`.

**Methods**
- **Avalanche:** all spare money to the highest rate first. Cheapest mathematically.
- **Snowball:** smallest balance first. Costs slightly more, but early wins keep some people going. Use it when motivation is the problem.

**Tools**
- **Umschuldung:** replace an overdraft or expensive loan with a cheaper instalment loan. Compare with "schufaneutral" condition enquiries, which do not hurt the score.
- **Early repayment:** consumer loans can be repaid early at any time; the bank's fee is capped at 1% of the amount repaid (0.5% if less than a year remains).
- **Withdrawal:** consumer loan contracts can be revoked within 14 days.
- **Lower the Dispo limit** once it is cleared.
- **Student debt:** BAföG is interest-free with capped repayment; KfW study loans carry variable interest and are worth clearing earlier.

**When debt is out of control**
- Free, reputable debt counselling: municipal Schuldnerberatung, Caritas, Diakonie, AWO, Verbraucherzentrale. Avoid commercial "debt regulators" charging upfront fees.
- **P-Konto:** converts a current account into one with a protected basic amount each month during garnishment (about €1,500 plus more for dependants; verify the current figure).
- **Privatinsolvenz:** discharge of remaining debts normally after three years.

## Schufa and credit standing

- One free data copy a year under Art. 15 GDPR (order at meineschufa.de; the paid products are unnecessary).
- Check it before applying for a mortgage; have wrong or outdated entries corrected.
- What helps: paying on time, few accounts and cards, no frequent loan applications. Condition enquiries are neutral; credit applications are not.

## Insurance

Insure what would ruin the household; pay small losses from savings.

| Priority | Cover | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Essential | Health insurance | Compulsory. Statutory or private; see below. |
| Essential | Privathaftpflicht | Everyone. Family tariff covers partner and children. Sum insured at least €10 million. |
| Essential for earners | Berufsunfähigkeit (BU) | Anyone who lives off their work. Cheaper and easier to get when young and healthy. Insure about 60–80% of net income to at least age 65–67. Answer health questions completely. |
| Situation | Risikolebensversicherung | Dependants or a joint mortgage. Cover the loan plus several years of income. Unmarried couples: insure each other "crosswise" to avoid inheritance tax. |
| Situation | Wohngebäude with Elementarschäden | Homeowners. |
| Situation | Kfz-Haftpflicht | Compulsory with a car; Kasko depends on the car's value. |
| Often sensible | Hausrat, Auslandsreise-Krankenversicherung, Rechtsschutz, Pflegezusatz | Depends on belongings, travel and risk. |
| Usually not worth it | Phone, glasses, luggage, appliance warranties, Sterbegeld, Insassenunfall, accident insurance with premium refund | Small or unlikely losses, expensive relative to cover. |

- If BU is unaffordable or declined, look at Erwerbsunfähigkeit, Grundfähigkeit or Dread-Disease cover as second-best.
- Never combine insurance with saving in one product (endowment life, pension policy with BU rider tied to it) unless the cost has been checked: separate products are usually cheaper and more flexible.
- Buy through comparison and, for BU, through an independent broker or fee-based adviser who submits anonymous pre-enquiries.

## Statutory or private health insurance

- Employees can switch to private (PKV) only above the Versicherungspflichtgrenze: €77,400 a year in 2026, expected €84,150 in 2027. The self-employed and civil servants can choose freely.
- Statutory: contributions follow income (up to the ceiling), family members without income are covered free, contributions fall in retirement.
- Private: premiums follow age and health at entry, each family member pays, premiums rise over time and do not fall with income in retirement.
- Returning to statutory insurance is difficult after 55. Treat a switch as a decision for life, not as a monthly saving.
- Within the statutory system, changing fund is easy and can save several hundred euros a year: the Zusatzbeitrag ranged from about 2.2% to over 4% in 2026.

## Consumer rights worth knowing

- 14-day right of withdrawal for most online and distance contracts, including loans and insurance (30 days for life insurance).
- Contracts that renew automatically can be cancelled monthly after the first term.
- A Depot transfer to another bank is free.
- Banks must offer a basic account (Basiskonto) to everyone.
