# Family, partnership and passing on wealth

Headline figures (tax tariff, social insurance values, pension values, subsidies, allowances) were checked against sources on 11 November 2026. Rules of thumb and long-standing legal rules come from general knowledge and should be confirmed before anyone relies on them. This area is legally heavy: give orientation, then refer to a Notar, a Fachanwalt für Erbrecht or a Steuerberater.

## Couples

- **Unmarried partners** are strangers in tax and inheritance law: no splitting, a €20,000 inheritance allowance, no automatic right to decide for each other. They need wills, powers of attorney, and for joint property a written agreement on shares and exit.
- **Marriage or registered partnership** brings joint assessment (splitting), a €500,000 allowance, survivor's pension rights and pension sharing on divorce.
- **Default property regime (Zugewinngemeinschaft):** each keeps their own assets; only the gain during the marriage is equalised on divorce or death. A marriage contract is worth considering for business owners, large differences in wealth, and second marriages.
- **Money between partners:** agree who pays what, and make sure the lower earner or the one reducing hours for children keeps building their own pension and investments. Pension points for child-raising periods (up to three years per child) should be assigned to the right parent.

## Children

- Kindergeld €259 a month per child; alternatively the Kinderfreibetrag for higher earners (see `tax.md`).
- **Elterngeld:** roughly 65–67% of prior net income, between €300 and €1,800 a month for Basiselterngeld, for 12 months plus 2 partner months; ElterngeldPlus halves the amount and doubles the duration. An income limit applies to high-earning households (verify the current threshold). The tax class in the months before birth affects the amount.
- Childcare costs: 80% deductible, at most €4,800 per child.
- Long-term care contributions fall slightly from the second child.
- Altersvorsorgedepot child bonus of up to €300 per child per year from 2027; Frühstart-Rente for children born from 2020 (see `retirement.md`).
- **Saving for children:** in the parents' name keeps control and flexibility; in the child's name uses the child's tax allowances but the money belongs to the child at 18, counts for BAföG, and investment income above about €565 a month ends free family health insurance.
- A broad equity ETF fits an 18-year horizon. Shift to safe assets a few years before the money is needed.

## Protecting the family

- Risikolebensversicherung for anyone others depend on, sized to the mortgage plus several years of income.
- BU for the earners.
- Privathaftpflicht family tariff.
- Guardianship instruction (Sorgerechtsverfügung) naming who should care for minor children.

## Powers of attorney and directives

Without these, even a spouse cannot fully act for an adult who loses capacity (the emergency representation right between spouses is limited to health matters and six months).
- **Vorsorgevollmacht:** names who handles finances, health and housing decisions. Banks often want their own form in addition.
- **Patientenverfügung:** medical wishes.
- **Betreuungsverfügung:** names a preferred court-appointed guardian if needed.
- Register them with the Zentrales Vorsorgeregister.

## Inheritance and gifts

**Who inherits without a will**
- Spouse and children share; without children, the spouse shares with parents or siblings of the deceased. Unmarried partners and stepchildren inherit nothing without a will.
- Close family members excluded by a will still have a claim to a compulsory share in cash (Pflichtteil: half of the statutory share).

**Wills**
- Handwritten in full, dated and signed, or notarised. Typed and signed is invalid.
- Married couples can write a joint will (Berliner Testament: spouse first, children after). Simple, but the children's allowances after the first death go unused, which matters for larger estates.
- Deposit the will with the probate court so it is found.

**Tax allowances, renewable every 10 years**

| Recipient | Allowance |
|---|---:|
| Spouse or registered partner | €500,000 |
| Child, stepchild (from each parent) | €400,000 |
| Grandchild (if the parent is alive) | €200,000 |
| Grandchild (if the parent has died) | €400,000 |
| Parents and grandparents who inherit | €100,000 |
| Siblings, nieces, nephews, unmarried partners, others | €20,000 |

- Tax rates above the allowance run from 7% to 50% depending on the relationship and amount.
- A surviving spouse and children additionally get a pension allowance on inheritance.
- **Family home:** passes tax-free on death to a spouse, or to children for up to 200 m² of living space, if they move in and stay for 10 years. Between living spouses the family home can be gifted tax-free.
- **Gifts during life:** because allowances renew every 10 years, early and repeated gifts transfer far more tax-free. Property is often gifted with a retained right of residence or usufruct (Nießbrauch), which also lowers the taxable value.
- **Reform under discussion:** proposals include replacing the 10-year renewal with a lifetime allowance. Check the status before planning around the current rules.
- Report gifts and inheritances to the tax office within three months.

## Caring for parents

- Children are liable for parents' care costs only above €100,000 of annual gross income per child.
- Long-term care insurance covers part of care costs; the gap for a nursing home place is typically well over €2,000 a month out of pocket (verify regionally).
- A private Pflegezusatzversicherung is worth a look in the forties and fifties.
