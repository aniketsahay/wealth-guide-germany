# Retirement planning in Germany

Headline figures (tax tariff, social insurance values, pension values, subsidies, allowances) were checked against sources on 11 November 2026. Rules of thumb and long-standing legal rules come from general knowledge and should be confirmed before anyone relies on them.

## The three layers (Schichten)

| Layer | Products | Tax while saving | Tax at payout | Access |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Basic | Statutory pension, professional schemes (Versorgungswerke), Rürup | Contributions deductible | Taxed (rising share, fully from 2058) | Lifelong pension only |
| 2 Subsidised | bAV, Riester, Altersvorsorgedepot (from 2027) | Subsidy or deduction | Fully taxed | From about 62–65 |
| 3 Private | ETF Depot, private pension insurance, property | From taxed income | Capital gains tax, or half of the gain for qualifying insurance | Any time (Depot) |

A sound plan usually has something in each layer. Layer 3 in an ETF Depot is the flexible core; layers 1 and 2 are worth using where the subsidy or tax saving clearly outweighs the lock-in and the later tax.

## Statutory pension (gesetzliche Rentenversicherung)

- Contribution 18.6% of gross up to the ceiling (€101,400 in 2026), half from the employer.
- One year at average earnings (provisional 2026: €51,944) earns one Entgeltpunkt. Earning double the average earns two; the ceiling caps it at just under two.
- Monthly pension = points × Rentenwert (€42.52 since 1 July 2026) × adjustment for early or late retirement.
- Pension level held at 48% of the average wage until 2031. After that it is expected to fall; plan with today's figure and no real growth.
- Regelaltersgrenze: 67 for anyone born in 1964 or later. Early retirement from 63 with 35 contribution years costs 0.3% per month, permanently. With 45 years, retirement without deduction is possible two years before the standard age.
- From age 50, extra payments can buy back deductions for planned early retirement; they are deductible like other pension contributions.
- What comes off the gross pension: health and care contributions (about 12–13% for statutory members) and income tax on the taxable share (84% for pensions starting in 2026, rising half a point a year).
- **Renteninformation:** sent yearly from age 27. Use the figure "at standard retirement age without future adjustments". All claims in one place: rentenuebersicht.de. Check the Versicherungsverlauf for gaps (school, study, child-raising periods).
- **Aktivrente (since 2026):** employees working past the standard age can earn up to €2,000 a month tax-free. Not for the self-employed.
- **Self-employed:** most are not compulsorily insured. Voluntary contributions are possible; some professions are compulsory (teachers, artists via KSK, craftspeople); liberal professions use Versorgungswerke.
- **Civil servants:** receive a Pension instead; out of scope for the gap calculation except as a known figure.

## The pension gap

1. Target income in retirement: default 80% of current net income, or spending minus costs that end (mortgage, commuting, children).
2. Subtract the expected net statutory pension (gross figure from the Renteninformation less about 12–13% contributions and estimated tax).
3. Subtract other secured pensions (bAV, Riester, Rürup, Versorgungswerk).
4. The rest is the gap, in today's euros.
5. Capital needed at retirement ≈ annual gap ÷ 0.03 to 0.035 (a 3–3.5% withdrawal rate), or ÷ 0.04 for a shorter retirement.
6. Convert to a monthly savings amount with the real-return assumptions in `SKILL.md`.

Example: target €2,800 net a month, expected net statutory pension €1,700, bAV €200. Gap €900 a month = €10,800 a year. Capital needed ≈ €10,800 ÷ 0.035 ≈ €309,000 in today's money.

## bAV (betriebliche Altersvorsorge)

- Every employee has a right to salary conversion (Entgeltumwandlung).
- Free of tax and social contributions up to 4% of the pension ceiling (2026: €4,056 a year, €338 a month). A further 4% is tax-free only (total €8,112).
- The employer must add 15% of the converted amount where it saves social contributions. Many add more, or pay a pension entirely themselves.

**Decision framework**
- Employer pays in without any salary conversion → always take it.
- Employer adds 20% or more to converted salary, or matches → usually worth it, at least up to the matched amount.
- Only the statutory 15%, an insurance product with high costs and a low guaranteed rate → compare with a private ETF plan; often the ETF plan wins for younger savers.
- Earnings above the health ceiling (€69,750 in 2026): no social contributions are saved on that part, but none are lost in the statutory pension either; the tax deferral remains.

**Catches**
- Payout is fully taxable. Statutory health members pay full health and care contributions on it (both halves) above an allowance of €197.75 a month in 2026.
- Converted salary earns no statutory pension points and lowers Elterngeld, ALG I and Krankengeld.
- On changing jobs the contract can usually be taken along or continued privately, but conditions may worsen. Ask for the costs and the transfer rules in writing.

## Altersvorsorgedepot (from 1 January 2027)

- Replaces Riester for new contracts. Law passed in spring 2026; check which certified products are on sale.
- **Subsidy:** 50 cents per euro on the first €360 a year, then 25 cents per euro up to €1,800. Maximum €540 a year.
- **Child bonus:** one euro per euro up to €300 per child per year. Full bonus from €25 a month.
- **Young savers:** one-off €200 when the first contract starts before age 25.
- No subsidy below €120 a year of own contributions.
- Own contributions up to €1,800 plus subsidies are deductible as Sonderausgaben where that is worth more than the subsidy (the tax office checks). Up to €6,840 a year can be paid in.
- No capital guarantee needed: up to 100% in funds and ETFs. The Standarddepot is capped at 1.0% effective cost a year.
- Open to employees and, for the first time, the self-employed.
- No tax during the saving phase. Payouts are taxed as income.
- Payout from 65 at the earliest and 70 at the latest: lifelong pension, or a payout plan running to at least 85. Up to 30% as a lump sum at the start.

| Own contribution per year | Subsidy (no children) | Subsidy with 2 children |
|---:|---:|---:|
| €120 | €60 | €300 |
| €300 | €150 | €750 |
| €360 | €180 | €780 |
| €1,200 | €390 | €990 |
| €1,800 | €540 | €1,140 |

**Who benefits most:** parents (the child bonus doubles or triples small contributions), low and middle earners (subsidy is a high percentage), and high earners through the deduction. **Trade-off:** money is locked until 65 and fully taxed at payout, whereas an ordinary Depot stays accessible and only the gain is taxed. For most people: fund the Altersvorsorgedepot up to the subsidised amount, then invest the rest in an ordinary Depot.

## Riester (existing contracts)

- New contracts possible only until the end of 2026. Existing contracts keep their terms.
- Subsidy: €175 a year basic, €300 per child born from 2008 (€185 before). Full subsidy needs 4% of the previous year's gross income paid in, counting the subsidies, minimum €60. Up to €2,100 including subsidies is deductible.
- **What to do with an old contract**
  1. Find the cost ratio and the guaranteed pension in the annual statement.
  2. Many children or low income with full subsidies → often worth continuing.
  3. High costs and poor returns → consider making it paid-up or switching into the new system once products exist. Switching is voluntary.
  4. Cancelling outright means repaying all subsidies and tax advantages. Rarely the best option.
  5. Wohn-Riester: capital can be used for an owner-occupied home.

## Rürup (Basisrente)

- Contributions 100% deductible up to €30,826 (€61,652 jointly) in 2026. For employees the statutory pension contributions of employee and employer count toward the cap first.
- Lifelong pension only, from 62 at the earliest. No lump sum, no surrender, cannot be pledged, limited inheritance (only via agreed survivor cover).
- Payouts taxed like the statutory pension.
- **Fits:** self-employed people without a compulsory scheme; high earners at or near the 42% rate who are several years from retirement; people aged 50+ making large one-off payments.
- **Does not fit:** anyone who may need the capital, low earners, people likely to leave Germany.
- Prefer low-cost net tariffs with ETFs. Reinvest the tax refund, otherwise the comparison with a private Depot is lost.

## Private pension insurance (layer 3)

- For contracts since 2012: if paid out after age 62 and after at least 12 years, only half of the gain is taxed at the personal rate. For fund-based policies 15% of fund gains is exempt first.
- As a monthly pension only a small "Ertragsanteil" is taxed (17% at age 67).
- Useful mainly for large sums, long horizons, frequent fund switching without tax, and inheritance planning. Only with low-cost net tariffs; classic commission tariffs rarely beat a Depot.

## Frühstart-Rente

- €10 a month from the state into a pension Depot for each child from age 6 until 18, starting with children born in 2020. Parents can add their own money.
- Passed by the Bundestag on 16 October 2026; intended start 1 January 2027. Confirm Bundesrat approval and the rules on private top-ups.
- Children born in 2019 or earlier receive no state money.

## VL and savings bonuses

- Employers pay up to €40 a month as vermögenswirksame Leistungen; check the contract or collective agreement. Unclaimed VL is lost pay.
- Arbeitnehmersparzulage: 20% on up to €400 a year into equity funds (€80) and 9% on up to €470 into a Bausparvertrag (€43), if taxable income is at most €40,000 (€80,000 jointly). Seven-year lock.
- Wohnungsbauprämie: 10% on up to €700 (€1,400 jointly) of own Bauspar contributions if taxable income is at most €35,000 (€70,000).

## Health insurance in retirement

- Statutory members who were insured for 90% of the second half of their working life join the KVdR: contributions only on pensions and bAV, not on capital income or rent.
- Otherwise (voluntary members), contributions are charged on all income including capital and rental income. Check eligibility in the fifties, while it can still be influenced.
- Private insurance premiums do not fall with income in retirement. Plan them as a fixed cost.

## Financial independence and early retirement

- Target capital: 25–30 times annual spending; plan with a 3–3.5% withdrawal rate for a retirement of 40 years or more.
- Before the statutory pension starts, the investor pays voluntary health contributions on all income, with a minimum contribution even at low income.
- Fewer contribution years mean a smaller statutory pension and possibly losing the 35- and 45-year thresholds.
- Withdrawal order: taxable Depot first (uses the Sparerpauschbetrag and low personal rates via Günstigerprüfung), locked products from 62–65, statutory pension last.

## Priority ladder by profile

| Profile | Order |
|---|---|
| Employee, employer contributes to bAV | bAV up to the match → Altersvorsorgedepot to the subsidy cap → ETF Depot |
| Employee, no employer extra | Altersvorsorgedepot to the subsidy cap → ETF Depot → bAV only after comparing costs |
| Parent, modest income | Altersvorsorgedepot at €25+ a month for the child bonus → emergency fund → ETF Depot |
| High earner near 42% | bAV match → ETF Depot → Rürup for part of the surplus → Altersvorsorgedepot via deduction |
| Self-employed | Disability cover → Rürup or voluntary statutory pension for a lifelong base → ETF Depot → Altersvorsorgedepot |
| Age 50+ | Close gaps in the Versicherungsverlauf → buy back deductions → Rürup one-offs → reduce equity share gradually |
