# Property: buying a home and renting out

Headline figures (tax tariff, social insurance values, pension values, subsidies, allowances) were checked against sources on 11 November 2026. Rules of thumb and long-standing legal rules come from general knowledge and should be confirmed before anyone relies on them. Mortgage rates, prices and KfW conditions move constantly: always look them up.

## Buy or rent

Neither is automatically better. Compare the full monthly cost of owning with rent, and invest the difference in the renting case.

**Monthly cost of owning** = interest + maintenance (about 1–1.5% of the building value a year) + non-recoverable running costs + the return the equity would have earned elsewhere. Repayment (Tilgung) is saving, not cost.

**Rules of thumb**
- Price ÷ annual net cold rent for a comparable home: under 20 is cheap, 20–25 moderate, above 25–30 expensive. In expensive cities renting and investing often wins on paper.
- Buying fits when the household expects to stay 10 years or more, income is stable, and the purchase does not use up the emergency fund.
- Buying forces saving through repayment. A renter only matches that by investing the difference every month.
- Buying costs are sunk: selling within a few years almost always loses money.

## What a purchase costs

| Item | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Grunderwerbsteuer | 3.5–6.5% of the price, by Land (below) |
| Notary and land registry | about 1.5–2% |
| Agent (Makler) | up to about 3.57% for the buyer; shared with the seller by law for homes |
| Renovation, kitchen, moving | case by case; get quotes before signing |

**Grunderwerbsteuer by Land**
- 3.5%: Bayern
- 5.0%: Baden-Württemberg, Niedersachsen, Rheinland-Pfalz, Sachsen-Anhalt, Thüringen
- 5.5%: Bremen, Hamburg, Sachsen
- 6.0%: Berlin, Hessen, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern
- 6.5%: Brandenburg, Nordrhein-Westfalen, Saarland, Schleswig-Holstein

Some Länder run grants for first-time buyers (for example Hessengeld). Movable items sold with the home (fitted kitchen, furniture) can be listed separately in the contract and are not subject to Grunderwerbsteuer, within reason.

## How much home is affordable

1. Monthly rate (interest plus repayment) plus running costs should stay at or below about 35–40% of net household income.
2. Equity: at least the purchase costs, ideally plus 10–20% of the price. More equity means a noticeably better rate.
3. Maximum loan ≈ affordable annual rate ÷ (interest rate + initial repayment rate).

Example: €4,500 net, 35% = €1,575 a month, less €350 running costs = €1,225 rate = €14,700 a year. At 3.8% interest and 2% repayment the maximum loan is about €14,700 ÷ 0.058 ≈ €253,000.

## Structuring the mortgage

- **Initial repayment:** at least 2%, better 3%. At 2% the loan runs well over 30 years.
- **Zinsbindung:** 10–15 years is usual. Longer costs a small premium and buys certainty; the borrower can still leave after 10 years.
- **Right to terminate:** 10 years after full payout, any fixed-rate loan can be ended with 6 months' notice and no fee (§ 489 BGB). For a follow-on loan the 10 years run from signing.
- **Sondertilgung:** 5% a year free of charge is common. Negotiate it, and the right to change the repayment rate.
- **Anschlussfinanzierung:** start comparing 12 months before the fixed rate ends. A Forward-Darlehen fixes a rate up to several years ahead for a premium. Never accept the existing bank's first offer without comparison.
- **Compare offers** from at least three sources, including a broker platform. Check effective rate, commitment interest (Bereitstellungszinsen), and flexibility.
- **Insure the risk:** Risikolebensversicherung for co-borrowers and dependants; BU for the main earner; Wohngebäudeversicherung with natural-hazard cover.

## Extra repayment or invest

Compare the mortgage rate with the expected after-tax return of the alternative.
- Extra repayment earns the mortgage rate, risk-free and tax-free.
- An equity ETF has a higher expected return with large swings, and gains are taxed.
- Sensible middle: keep the emergency fund intact, use employer and state subsidies, then split. Repay more when the mortgage rate is above about 4%, or when the fixed-rate period ends soon and the remaining debt is large.

## KfW loans and grants

Conditions and income limits change often; check kfw.de and apply through a bank before signing the purchase contract.
- **300 Wohneigentum für Familien:** reduced-rate loan for families building or buying new, energy-efficient homes; income limits apply.
- **308 Jung kauft Alt:** families buying an existing home in energy class F, G or H and renovating it. Up to €180,000 since 3 August 2026; individual measures now suffice.
- **124 Wohneigentumsprogramm:** general loan for owner-occupiers.
- **297/298 Klimafreundlicher Neubau.**
- **261 and BEG grants:** energy renovation; heating replacement subsidies.
- Länder development banks (for example NRW.BANK, BayernLabo, IBB) add their own programmes.

## Renting out (Kapitalanlage)

**Yield**
- Gross yield = annual net cold rent ÷ purchase price.
- Net yield = (rent − non-recoverable costs − maintenance reserve) ÷ (price + purchase costs).
- Compare the net yield with the mortgage rate. If it is lower, the investment depends on price growth or tax effects.

**Tax**
- Rent is taxed at the personal rate after deducting loan interest, running costs, maintenance and depreciation.
- Depreciation (AfA) on the building share only: 2% a year for buildings completed 1925–2022, 3% from 2023, 2.5% before 1925. New residential buildings with construction start or purchase contract between 1 October 2023 and 30 September 2029 can use 5% declining-balance instead.
- Renovation costing more than 15% of the building's purchase price (net of VAT) within three years of purchase must be depreciated, not deducted at once.
- Losses from renting offset salary income.
- Sale is tax-free after 10 years of ownership. Within 10 years the gain is taxed at the personal rate.
- A home the owner lived in during the year of sale and the two years before can be sold tax-free at any time.

**Risks to state plainly**
- One flat is a concentrated, leveraged, illiquid investment in one location.
- Vacancy, non-paying tenants, special levies in owners' associations, rent regulation (Mietpreisbremse, Kappungsgrenze), energy-efficiency requirements.
- It is a small business: time, paperwork and tenant law.
- Always compare with the same equity in a diversified ETF portfolio.

## Other points

- **Grundsteuer** was reformed from 2025; amounts vary strongly by municipality.
- **Wohn-Riester and Bausparen:** Bausparverträge fix a future loan rate; useful mainly for a known renovation or follow-on financing need, not as an investment.
- **Erbbaurecht (leasehold), auctions, buying with partners while unmarried:** legally complex; refer to a Notar or lawyer.
- **Inherited or gifted property:** see `family-estate.md`.
