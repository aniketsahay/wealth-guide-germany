# Investing from Germany

Headline figures (tax tariff, social insurance values, pension values, subsidies, allowances) were checked against sources on 11 November 2026. Rules of thumb and long-standing legal rules come from general knowledge and should be confirmed before anyone relies on them. Fund names are examples of a category, never recommendations. Check the current TER and Sparplan availability on justetf.com before naming a fund in a plan.

## Principles

1. **Costs and diversification are the two things an investor controls.** Broad, cheap index funds beat most active funds after costs over long periods (Bogle; Malkiel; Kommer for the German context).
2. **Time in the market, not timing.** Money needed within about five years does not belong in equities.
3. **Risk is set by the split between equities and safe assets,** not by picking cleverer equity funds.
4. **Automate.** A monthly Sparplan on payday removes the decision.
5. **Behaviour is the largest cost.** Selling in a crash turns a temporary loss into a permanent one. Size the equity share so a 40–50% fall is survivable.

## Asset allocation

| Risk tolerance | Equity share of long-term money | Safe share |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 30–50% | 50–70% |
| Balanced | 60–70% | 30–40% |
| Growth | 80–100% | 0–20% |

Adjust for capacity as well as tolerance:
- Lower the equity share for a short horizon, unstable income, a single earner with dependants, or a planned home purchase.
- Raise it for a long horizon, secure income (civil servants, tenured staff), or a large safe statutory pension claim, which itself behaves like a bond.
- The emergency fund is separate and never counts as the safe share.

## Model portfolios

**One-fund portfolio (default for beginners)**
- 100% of the equity share in a global all-cap or large/mid-cap ETF covering developed and emerging markets.

**Two-fund portfolio**
- About 70–80% developed markets (MSCI World) and 20–30% emerging markets (MSCI EM IMI). More control over the emerging-markets weight; needs rebalancing.

**With a safe part**
- Either of the above for the equity share, plus Tagesgeld, Festgeld ladder, short-dated Bundesanleihen or a euro money-market ETF for the safe share.

Leave out, for nearly everyone: sector and theme funds, single-country bets, leveraged products, and more than about 5–10% in crypto or gold combined.

## Choosing an ETF

- **Index:** broad (FTSE All-World, MSCI ACWI, MSCI ACWI IMI, MSCI World).
- **Cost:** TER roughly 0.07–0.20% for world funds in 2026; also compare tracking difference.
- **Size and age:** above about €100 million and more than a year old, so closure is unlikely.
- **Domicile:** Ireland or Luxembourg (UCITS). Irish funds reduce US withholding tax inside the fund.
- **Replication:** physical or synthetic are both acceptable; physical is easier to explain.
- **Accumulating or distributing:** see the tax section.
- **Availability:** a free or cheap Sparplan at the user's broker often decides between otherwise equal funds.

## Safe assets

- **Tagesgeld / Festgeld:** statutory deposit protection of €100,000 per person per bank across the EU. Above that, spread across banks. Prefer countries with strong ratings; promotional rates usually last only a few months.
- **Festgeld ladder:** split into equal parts maturing in 1, 2 and 3 years to keep access and average the rate.
- **Bundesanleihen** held to maturity, or a short-dated euro government bond ETF.
- **Euro money-market ETF:** tracks the overnight rate, tradeable daily, no deposit protection but very low risk.

## Broker and account

- A German-domiciled broker or bank withholds tax automatically and issues the Jahressteuerbescheinigung. A foreign broker leaves all reporting to the investor (Anlage KAP).
- Compare: Sparplan fee, order fee, custody fee, range of ETFs, negative-balance interest. Do not rank brokers from memory; point to current comparisons (Finanztip, Stiftung Warentest).
- Moving a Depot to another bank is free by law and is not a sale for tax.
- Joint Depot for couples is possible; each partner's Sparerpauschbetrag applies with a joint Freistellungsauftrag only if they are taxed jointly.

## Tax on investments

- **Abgeltungsteuer:** 25% plus 5.5% Soli = 26.375%; about 27.8–28% with church tax, which the bank withholds automatically.
- **Sparerpauschbetrag:** €1,000 per person, €2,000 for couples taxed jointly. Needs a Freistellungsauftrag at each bank; the total across banks must not exceed the allowance.
- **Teilfreistellung:** 30% of distributions and gains from equity funds (over 50% equities) is tax-free; 15% for mixed funds (at least 25% equities); 60% for property funds. Effective rate on equity-ETF gains is about 18.5%.
- **Vorabpauschale** (accumulating funds): value on 1 January × Basiszins × 0.7, capped at the year's actual gain, minus distributions. Basiszins 2026: 3.20% (2025: 2.53%).
  - Example: €50,000 in an accumulating equity ETF on 1 January 2026, fund rises during the year. Base amount €50,000 × 3.20% × 0.7 = €1,120. After 30% Teilfreistellung €784 is taxable. If the allowance is already used, tax is €784 × 26.375% ≈ €207, debited in early January 2027. It is credited against tax when the fund is eventually sold.
  - Keep cash on the broker's settlement account in January.
- **FIFO:** on a sale, the oldest units are treated as sold first.
- **Loss pots:** losses on individual shares can only be offset against gains on individual shares. Fund and ETF losses go into the general pot and offset any capital income. A Verlustbescheinigung (request by 15 December) moves a loss to the tax return so it can offset gains at another bank.
- **Günstigerprüfung:** if the personal marginal rate is below 25%, declare capital income in the return and reclaim the difference. Relevant for students, pensioners, parental leave and children's Depots.
- **NV-Bescheinigung:** for people with very low total income, exempts capital income from withholding altogether.

### Using the allowance well

- A distributing world ETF yields roughly 1.5–2% a year, so about €70,000–95,000 in a distributing equity ETF fills one person's €1,000 allowance (after 30% Teilfreistellung, €1,430 of distributions are covered). Above that, accumulating is slightly more efficient.
- Alternatively realise up to about €1,430 of equity-ETF gains per person each year and rebuy ("tax-gain harvesting"). Worth it only if trading costs are low.
- Couples taxed jointly: hold assets so both allowances are used.
- Children: a Depot in the child's name uses the child's Grundfreibetrag and Sparerpauschbetrag. The money then belongs to the child irrevocably, counts as the child's assets for BAföG, and investment income above about €565 a month (2026) ends free family health insurance.

## Crypto and gold

- Private sale under § 23 EStG: tax-free after more than one year; within a year taxed at the personal rate. Gains under €1,000 a year in total are exempt (a threshold, not an allowance: at €1,000 everything is taxable).
- **Pending:** a finance ministry draft (September 2026) would end the one-year rule for crypto bought after 31 December 2026 and tax it like other capital income. Not adopted by the cabinet as of early November 2026. Check status every time.
- Physical gold and gold products with a delivery claim follow the same one-year rule today.
- Treat both as satellites: at most about 5–10% of invested assets combined, and only money the investor can afford to see halve.

## What to avoid or check carefully

- Endowment and unit-linked life or pension policies with high acquisition costs; compare the effective cost to an ETF plan.
- Actively managed funds with an Ausgabeaufschlag of several percent.
- Bausparverträge as an investment (they are a financing tool).
- Certificates, CFDs, options and leveraged ETFs.
- "Guaranteed" high returns, unsolicited tips, and anything the investor cannot explain in two sentences.
- **Existing policies:** never recommend cancelling without checking surrender value, guaranteed interest (old contracts can carry 2.25–4%), tax status (contracts before 2005 can be tax-free at payout), and subsidies that would be reclaimed. Making a contract paid-up (beitragsfrei) is often better than cancelling.

## Lump sum, rebalancing, withdrawal

- **Lump sum:** investing at once has the higher expected return; spreading over 6–12 months is acceptable if it prevents regret. Decide once and automate.
- **Rebalancing:** once a year, or when the equity share drifts more than about 5 points from target. Use new contributions first to avoid taxable sales.
- **Withdrawal phase:** a 3–3.5% initial withdrawal rate is a reasonable planning figure for long retirements from Germany; keep two to three years of spending in safe assets; sell fund units monthly or quarterly (Entnahmeplan), which also uses the Sparerpauschbetrag every year.
