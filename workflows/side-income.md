# Workflow: starting a side income

Tax and social insurance rules are in `knowledge/income-career.md`.

## Step 1: choose (weeks 1–2)

1. List skills others already pay for in the main job or hobbies.
2. Pick one offer and one type of customer. Estimate price, hours available per week and realistic monthly revenue.
3. Target: net profit per hour after tax at least as high as the main job's net hourly pay, or a clear path to it.
4. Check the employment contract: report the side work, and make sure it does not compete with the employer.

## Step 2: register (weeks 2–4)

1. Decide the form: freiberuflich (no trade registration) or Gewerbe (register at the local Gewerbeamt for a small fee). If unsure, ask the tax office.
2. Complete the "Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung" in ELSTER. A tax number follows in a few weeks.
3. Choose Kleinunternehmer status if turnover will stay under €25,000 a year and customers are mostly private.
4. Open a separate current account for the business.
5. Tell the health fund if the side work could exceed about 20 hours a week or the main job's income.
6. Check whether the activity is subject to compulsory pension insurance (teaching and coaching often are).

## Step 3: first customers (months 1–3)

1. Start with the existing network; one paying customer teaches more than a finished website.
2. Invoice correctly from the first job: name and address of both parties, tax number, date, consecutive invoice number, description, amount, and for Kleinunternehmer the note that no VAT is shown under § 19 UStG. Businesses must be able to receive e-invoices.
3. Keep every receipt. Simple bookkeeping software is enough.
4. Liability insurance for the activity if a mistake could cost a customer money.

## Step 4: money routine (monthly)

1. Move 30–45% of each payment to a tax reserve on Tagesgeld (the higher figure for higher main salaries).
2. Pay yourself a fixed amount; leave the rest as a buffer.
3. Invest side-income profit by Sparplan once the tax reserve and emergency fund are funded.

## Step 5: taxes (yearly)

1. Income tax return with Anlage S (freelance) or G (trade) and the cash-basis profit statement (Anlage EÜR). Filing is compulsory with side profit above €410.
2. Kleinunternehmer: annual VAT return only where the tax office requires it; watch the €25,000 and €100,000 limits.
3. Expect advance payments (Vorauszahlungen) to be set after the first assessment.
4. Deduct: equipment, software, proportionate phone and internet, travel, training, home office where the conditions are met.
5. Gewerbe: trade tax only above €24,500 profit.

## Step 6: decide after 6–12 months

- **Grow:** raise prices first, then hours. Consider leaving Kleinunternehmer status if customers are businesses.
- **Hold:** keep as a stable extra, invested automatically.
- **Stop:** deregister with the tax office and, for a Gewerbe, the Gewerbeamt.
- **Go full-time:** only with 6–12 months of spending in reserve, health and disability cover arranged, and a retirement plan (see `knowledge/retirement.md`).

## Pitfalls

- No tax reserve, then a back-payment plus advance payments in the same year.
- Crossing the Kleinunternehmer limit unnoticed: VAT is due from that sale onward.
- One client providing nearly all revenue (risk of being treated as an employee, or of compulsory pension insurance).
- Under-pricing: the hourly rate has to cover tax, insurance, unpaid time and holidays.
