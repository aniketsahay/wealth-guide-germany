# Workflow: getting out of debt

For anyone with overdraft, card or instalment debt. Benchmarks are in `knowledge/money-basics.md`.

## Day 1: see everything
1. List every debt: lender, balance, interest rate, monthly payment, end date. Include buy-now-pay-later plans and money owed to family.
2. Get the free Schufa data copy and compare.
3. Add up the interest paid per month. This is the number to shrink.

## Week 1: stop the bleeding
1. Build a one-month reserve first, so the next surprise does not go back on the overdraft.
2. Cut or cancel subscriptions and contracts; switch electricity, gas, mobile and car insurance where notice periods allow.
3. Arrears on rent, energy or health insurance come before everything else: call the creditor and agree instalments in writing.

## Week 2: make it cheaper
1. Replace overdraft and card balances with one instalment loan at a lower rate, then reduce or close the overdraft limit.
2. Ask existing lenders for early-repayment terms; consumer loans can usually be repaid early for a small capped fee.
3. Compare refinancing offers with a non-binding enquiry ("Konditionsanfrage") so the Schufa score is not affected.

## Month 1 onward: pay down
1. Pay the minimum on everything, then put every spare euro on the debt with the highest rate (avalanche). If motivation is the problem, clear the smallest balance first (snowball).
2. Put windfalls (tax refund, bonus, gifts) straight on the debt.
3. Keep taking employer money (bAV contribution, VL) but pause other investing until debt costing more than about 5–6% is gone.

## If it does not add up
Payments exceed what is left after basic living costs, or collectors and court letters arrive: go to free debt counselling (municipal, Caritas, Diakonie, Verbraucherzentrale). Convert the current account to a protected account (P-Konto). Do not pay commercial "debt regulators" up-front fees.

## Done when
No overdraft or card debt, remaining loans under about 5% interest, three months of spending in reserve. Then start `first-investment.md`.
